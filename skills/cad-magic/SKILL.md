# CAD Magic

1. Identify the deliverable, units, and dimensions that matter. Separate measured or specified dimensions from visual estimates. If a drawing disagrees with itself, record which dimension governs rather than silently rounding away the discrepancy.
2. Choose a sketch plane and stable origin. Express design intent through dimensions and relations in sketch-based CAD, or through shared parameters and explicit references in code-based CAD. Reuse the existing model's toolchain; for new headless exercises, CadQuery is a demonstrated option.
3. Build the main volumes before details: extrude profiles, cut recesses, then add edge treatments where appropriate. Keep editable source separate from STEP exports. Don't describe a code-based profile as solver-verified or a STEP file as an editable feature history.
4. Verify the expected solid count, geometric validity, and critical dimensions. Change a meaningful parameter and rebuild to test an intended invariant, such as concentricity. If exporting STEP, reimport and repeat the geometry checks.
5. When asked to show the result, render the actual saved geometry and inspect the image before sharing. Label independent display scales. For print work, check the intended scale in the slicer; CAD validity alone is not proof of printability.
6. Report artifacts, assumptions, checks run, and limits. For course work, distinguish what was read or watched from what was built and verified.

## Apply the workflow to the input

- **Dimensioned part:** Anchor the sketch or profile to the origin or a stable datum. Use dimensions and relations rather than fixing geometry where it was drawn. Change one driving dimension and check the intended invariant, such as concentric tiers after a diameter edit ([Lesson 1](../../lessons/01/README.md)).
- **Image or contour reference:** Inspect the original media first. Pick the landmarks, internal boundaries, or negative spaces that make the subject recognizable. Calibrate scale and origin before tracing. Compare those features with a matched view or section of the exported part; don't use a picture generated by the model as independent evidence. Sample curvature changes rather than pixel noise; inspect the closed spline for overshoot or self-intersection before extruding. Keep functional features such as hole diameters independently dimensioned. Verify terrain with sections and check bores separately from overall bounds.

- **Surface detail:** Model holes, pockets, and grooves that need to exist in the exported part. Keep appearance-only texture separate. After STEP reimport, check the opening and retained material beneath a blind cut, or confirm a through-hole with a section. Use angled light to reveal shallow details.

The [lesson index](../../lessons/README.md) provides evidence and rebuildable examples; the workflow above does not require reading the lessons first.

## Lesson documentation

Use the same structure for every lesson: title and course metadata; a brief account of what was reviewed and which CAD tool was used; then **What I learned**, **Course exercises**, **Practice results**, **Verification and limits**, **Artifacts and rebuild**, and **Sources**. Separate the instructor's exercise from the model actually built, link the editable source and exports, and record concrete checks and their limits. Credit MIT OpenCourseWare and the instructor with links to the lesson resources.

When a lesson demonstrates a reusable technique, add it to the workflow and record the specific evidence in the lesson index. Keep the root README focused on what the agent can do; put exercise details and rebuild instructions under the lesson. Do not advertise a new capability until its checks support it.
